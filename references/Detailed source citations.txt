### SLIDE 6

- IEA (International Energy Agency), "World Energy Outlook 2019":
https://iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/98909c1b-aabc-4797-9926-35307b418cdb/WEO2019-free.pdf
References: 7.2 Renewables overview (pages 305-306) and 7.5 Material efficiency in heavy industries (pages 311-319). The report states that steel, cement, and chemicals remain heavily dependent on fossil fuels; renewable electricity alone is insufficient, requiring new technologies and process conversions. 6.12 Exploring the new frontiers of flexibility (pages 292-298): it is explicitly stated that integrating renewables requires energy storage, grid flexibility, and new power system designs. Without these, renewables cannot replace fossil fuels.

- REN21, "Renewables 2019 Global Status Report": 
https://www.ren21.net/gsr-2019/chapters/chapter_01/chapter_01/
References: "Heating and Cooling": notes that while the majority of global energy demand is for heating & cooling, industry is the primary driver. Renewables cover electricity well but contribute very little to industrial heat. "Transport": sector still dominated by fossil fuels, with marginal renewable presence and structural limits for biofuels.


### SLIDE 7

- IRENA, "The energy transition in Brazil":
https://www.irena.org/-/media/Files/IRENA/Agency/Publication/2025/Nov/IRENA_COU_Energy_transition_Brazil_2025.pdf
Document Reference: Pages 13/14 confirm that their "transition" is actually the maintenance of a historically high share due to hydropower and biofuels. Pages 36/37 (Figure 21) show the growth of solar after 2020.

- European Commission, "Renewable Energy Progress Report 2019": 
https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:52019DC0225
Document Reference: Section on Member States' progress. Certifies the success of Italy and Germany in exceeding the interim targets set for 2020.

- IEA/OECD, “An Energy Sector Roadmap to Carbon Neutrality in China”
https://iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/9448bd6e-670e-4cfd-953c-32e822a80f77/AnenergysectorroadmaptocarbonneutralityinChina.pdf
Document Reference: Pages 21-23 explain China's rapid development and that, despite efforts to transition to renewables, it remains overly dependent on coal. 

- MIT, "Climate Change, Directed Innovation, and Energy Transition":
https://economics.mit.edu/sites/default/files/2023-08/Climate%20Change%2C%20Directed%20Innovation%2C%20and%20Energy%20Transistion%20-%20The%20Long-run%20Consequences%20of%20the%20Shale%20Gas%20Revolution.pdf
Document Reference: Pages 3 to 5 contain the key graph: the share of clean energy patents in the US. It shows that as fracking exploded (2007-2012), renewable research in the US plummeted. Pages 30 to 45 ("Quantitative Evaluation" or "Calibration") apply the model to US power sector data. Using US EIA data, they conclude the "shale revolution" reduced US electricity CO2 intensity by 11.2% in the short term but created a technological "trap" that slows down future progress.
